
Evidence-led assessment and useful repair feedback

Keep all preceding validity rules, criterion weights, and approval thresholds. Assess
validity, realism, and opportunities for richness separately. Do not call a valid but
thin artifact invalid solely because it could be richer. Do not impose message, thread,
multi-hop, private-channel, or evidence-reuse quotas.

Catalog: examine the information needs across the collection, not just whether each
answer is present in the facts. Look for repeated reasoning under different names.
Identify natural opportunities for temporal scope, comparisons, exceptions, and joining
records across related workstreams. Ordinary single-fact lookups remain appropriate.
A diversity suggestion must identify specific tasks and explain the different information
need; do not make a simple question obscure merely to increase tool use.

World: investigate whether the workspace depicts work beyond delivering canonical
answers. Look for meaningful preparation, alternative proposals, decisions with reasons,
coordination dependencies, follow-up outcomes, and references to earlier work where the
scenario warrants them. These are possibilities, not a template every thread must follow.
Repeated acknowledgments, paraphrases of the same fact, and randomized timestamps do not
establish realism or richness. Conversely, concise or regularly scheduled real work is
not inherently defective. Cite the actual pattern and its effect before rejecting it.

For each blocking issue, use the existing defect/requested_change fields to state:
(1) specific evidence and affected IDs, (2) why it blocks validity or minimum world
quality, (3) the underlying change required, and (4) an observable acceptance condition.
Preserve supported answers, existing useful context, chronology, and retrieval validity.
A critique should guide a repair without writing the whole replacement or prescribing
the same dialogue structure to every thread. Deleting filler may solve duplication but
is not evidence that the remaining workspace has become substantively richer.

Use the existing summary field for a brief evidence-based assessment of strengths and
limitations, including concrete justification for unusually high or low criterion scores.
Below .75 means a concrete defect blocks minimum quality; .75 is minimally acceptable;
.80 means solid support on that criterion; .90 or above needs affirmative evidence of
strong quality rather than merely no detected error. These are anchors, not new weights.
Evidence composition concerns the complete requested answer; a legitimate single-message
answer does not need to be scattered across messages to earn a good score.

End summary with the heading "Nonblocking improvements:" followed by at most two
prioritized, specific suggestions, each naming affected IDs, a useful change, and how
to recognize success. If no useful suggestion is supported, write
"Nonblocking improvements: none." Suggestions must be proportionate, preserve validity,
and should improve actual work context or information needs rather than numeric proxies.
Keep suggestions out of issues: issues are blocking, while these suggestions do not
prevent approval. Use the same existing verdict schema. Do not add new fields.
